OS Theoretical Concepts & Algorithm Reference

First-Come, First-Served (FCFS) Non-Preemptive

Processes are executed strictly in order of arrival time using a simple FIFO queue.

  • Advantage: Minimal overhead, simple implementation.
  • Disadvantage: Vulnerable to Convoy Effect (short processes stuck behind a massive burst process).
Shortest Job First (SJF) Non-Preemptive

Selects the waiting process with the smallest CPU burst time.

  • Advantage: Mathematically optimal for minimizing average waiting time.
  • Disadvantage: Risk of Starvation for long processes; burst time must be known in advance.
Shortest Remaining Time First (SRTF) Preemptive

Preemptive version of SJF. Immediately preempts the running process if a new arrival has a shorter remaining burst time.

  • Advantage: Provides minimal turnaround times for short tasks.
  • Disadvantage: High context switching overhead and severe starvation risk for long processes.
Priority Scheduling (Non-Preemptive) Non-Preemptive

Allocates CPU to the process with highest priority value. Once started, runs to completion.

  • Advantage: Allows explicit process importance ranking.
  • Disadvantage: Starvation of low-priority processes (mitigated via Aging).
Priority Scheduling (Preemptive) Preemptive

Preempts CPU if a newly arrived process possesses higher priority than current executing process.

  • Advantage: Fast response time for critical high-priority tasks.
  • Disadvantage: Starvation risk & overhead due to preemptive context switching.
Round Robin (RR) Preemptive

Assigns fixed CPU Time Quantum (TQ) to processes in a circular queue.

  • Advantage: Fair CPU sharing, low response time, zero starvation.
  • Disadvantage: Performance heavily dependent on TQ (Too small = high overhead; Too large = degrades to FCFS).
